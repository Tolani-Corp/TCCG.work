# TCCG Federal Contracting Readiness Runbook

**Version:** 2026.1  
**Status:** Verification runbook — no identifier, certification, license or capacity is represented as active until supported by current evidence.

## 1. Entity-registration gate

### SAM.gov

For the legal entity that will bid directly on federal awards:

- maintain an active SAM.gov entity registration;
- verify the assigned Unique Entity ID (UEI);
- maintain accurate legal name, address, banking and taxpayer data;
- maintain current NAICS and representations/certifications;
- record registration expiration/renewal date;
- renew every 365 days to remain active.

Official: https://sam.gov/entity-registration

### CAGE

Verify the Commercial and Government Entity code associated with the bidding entity after SAM/agency processing. Store evidence and do not copy another affiliate's CAGE into TCCG materials.

## 2. Small-business profile

SBA's former Dynamic Small Business Search (DSBS) is now **Small Business Search (SBS)**. Maintain a complete searchable business profile after the applicable SBA/SAM account path is available.

Official portal guidance:
https://www.sba.gov/about-sba/open-government/about-sbagov-website/sba-account-login-registration-portals

Profile content should include only verified:

- capabilities;
- NAICS;
- geographic delivery capacity;
- certifications;
- keywords;
- quality/safety differentiators;
- past performance;
- points of contact.

## 3. Veteran certification gate

SBA VetCert is the current federal certification route for qualifying VOSBs/SDVOSBs. SBA states that SDVOSB certification enables qualifying firms to compete for federal SDVOSB sole-source and set-aside contracts, while certified VOSBs receive VA Vets First opportunities.

Official:
https://www.sba.gov/federal-contracting/contracting-assistance-programs/veteran-contracting-assistance-programs

Do not represent TCCG as VetCert-certified until:

- the exact bidding entity is eligible;
- ownership/control requirements are met;
- SAM registration is current;
- the certification is approved and active;
- the entity is searchable/verified through the current SBA systems.

## 4. NAICS and size standards

For every opportunity:

1. identify the solicitation's assigned NAICS;
2. confirm TCCG performs the required scope;
3. check the current SBA size standard;
4. assess affiliation where required;
5. document small-business status for the opportunity.

Initial TCCG focus codes in the Digital Launch registry:

- 236220 — Commercial and Institutional Building Construction
- 238220 — Plumbing, Heating, and Air-Conditioning Contractors
- 238210 — Electrical Contractors and Other Wiring Installation Contractors
- 238990 — All Other Specialty Trade Contractors
- 237310 — Highway, Street, and Bridge Construction, only where scope/team/licensing support the pursuit

The solicitation controls. Do not force an opportunity into a preferred NAICS.

## 5. Construction bonding gate

Federal construction bond requirements are solicitation- and value-dependent. FAR 28.102-1 currently requires performance and payment bonds for federal construction contracts exceeding $150,000. For construction contracts over $35,000 but not exceeding $150,000, the contracting officer selects specified payment protections.

Official:
https://www.acquisition.gov/far/28.102-1

Before proposal submission, record:

- expected bond requirement;
- bid bond requirement;
- performance/payment bond amounts;
- surety/agent confirmation;
- capacity impact;
- bond premium in estimate;
- power-of-attorney/execution process;
- deadline and electronic/original delivery requirements.

## 6. SBA Surety Bond Guarantee path

When conventional capacity is insufficient, assess the SBA SBG program through an SBA-authorized surety agent.

Official:

- https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/surety-bonds
- https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/surety-bonds/surety-bond-agency-directory
- https://www.sba.gov/partners/surety-bond-partners-agents/list-surety-bond-partners

The TCCG bonding plan is in `docs/risk/TCCG_BONDING_INSURANCE_READINESS.md`.

## 7. Solicitation compliance matrix

Create one row for every material instruction/requirement:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| requirement ID | unique reference |
| source | section / page / amendment |
| requirement | normalized instruction |
| owner | responsible contributor |
| response location | proposal section / attachment |
| evidence | registration, license, bond, plan, resume, project sheet, etc. |
| due date | contributor deadline |
| status | open / drafted / verified / approved |
| verifier | independent reviewer |
| exception | unresolved issue/deviation |

No proposal is authorized until all mandatory rows are either verified complete or explicitly dispositioned by authorized leadership.

## 8. Amendments and current-document control

- create a solicitation document index;
- log every amendment;
- update the compliance matrix when an amendment changes instructions, terms, wage determinations, scope, schedule or pricing basis;
- record acknowledgement requirements;
- prevent agents/estimators from using superseded solicitation documents by default.

## 9. Wage and labor review

For each construction solicitation, identify applicable labor clauses and wage determinations from the solicitation and current official sources. Assign payroll/compliance ownership before award.

Do not generalize wage rates from prior projects; use the contract's incorporated requirements.

## 10. Cybersecurity / information handling

Before receiving protected government information:

- identify FAR/DFARS or agency-specific clauses;
- determine whether CUI, FCI, export-controlled or security-sensitive information will be handled;
- establish the approved system boundary;
- verify required controls/attestations/certifications for that contract;
- restrict AI/RAG ingestion to approved environments;
- flow requirements to subcontractors where required;
- document incident/reporting obligations.

The public TCCG website and public email channel are not approved repositories for protected government information.

## 11. Proposal evidence room

Maintain controlled current evidence for:

- SAM registration;
- UEI;
- CAGE;
- SBA certifications;
- state/local licenses;
- W-9;
- insurance;
- surety/bonding letter;
- safety program/statistics where requested;
- quality program;
- key-person resumes;
- organization chart;
- project sheets;
- references;
- financial/capacity documentation where required;
- signed representations/letters;
- teaming/subcontracting agreements.

## 12. Past-performance attribution

Classify every reference as one of:

- TCCG corporate past performance;
- key-person prior experience;
- subcontract performance;
- teaming-partner experience.

Do not imply key-person or partner work was performed under a TCCG corporate contract.

## 13. Capture decision gate

### Bid

Proceed only when the pursuit has a credible path for:

- scope fit;
- license/jurisdiction fit;
- registration/certification eligibility;
- bonding/insurance;
- schedule and staffing;
- subcontractor/vendor coverage;
- cash-flow and working capital;
- contract risk;
- compliant price/proposal development;
- performance capacity.

### No-bid / team

If TCCG cannot responsibly prime the requirement, evaluate subcontracting, JV/teaming, mentor-protégé or supplier roles rather than overstating prime capability.

## 14. External readiness review

Use an APEX Accelerator as an independent, no-cost government-contracting readiness resource when useful. SAM.gov itself points registrants to APEX for free registration assistance.

Official:
https://www.apexaccelerators.us/

Recommended external review scope:

- entity registration;
- SBS profile;
- certification strategy;
- target agencies/NAICS;
- solicitation search/capture workflow;
- capability statement;
- subcontracting/teaming strategy;
- proposal compliance.
